Chapter X: Dead-Time Insertion for H-Bridge Protection
Introduction: The Necessity of Dead-Time
In any H-bridge or half-bridge topology used for motor control, each phase of the motor is driven by a pair of complementary switches—a High-Side (HS) switch connecting the phase to the positive power rail and a Low-Side (LS) switch connecting it to ground. In an ideal scenario, one switch would turn off at the exact same instant its complement turns on. However, physical transistors do not switch instantaneously; they have finite turn-on and turn-off times.
If a command is given to turn the LS switch on before the HS switch has fully turned off (or vice-versa), there will be a brief but catastrophic moment where both switches are simultaneously conducting. This creates a direct, low-impedance path from the power supply to ground through the two switches, a condition known as shoot-through. Shoot-through results in a massive current spike that can permanently damage the power switches, the driver IC, and even the power supply itself.
To prevent this destructive phenomenon, it is absolutely essential to implement dead-time. Dead-time is a short, deliberately inserted delay between the command to turn one switch OFF and the subsequent command to turn its complementary switch ON. During this interval, both the High-Side and Low-Side switches for that phase are guaranteed to be off, ensuring the "break-before-make" operation critical for hardware safety. The duration of this dead-time must be carefully chosen to be longer than the turn-off time of the transistors but as short as possible to minimize waveform distortion. For our design, this critical safety feature is software-programmable, allowing for fine-tuning based on the specific H-bridge characteristics.
Implementation Strategy: A Hierarchical, Two-Module Approach
To implement the dead-time logic in a clean, modular, and reusable manner, we adopted a hierarchical two-module design pattern. This approach separates the core single-phase logic from the top-level three-phase management, making the design easier to develop, test, and understand.
dead_zone_channel.v (The "Worker" Module): This module constitutes the core engine for dead-time insertion for a single motor phase. It is designed to take one "ideal" High-Side PWM signal as its primary input. Internally, it detects the rising and falling edges of this ideal signal. Upon detecting an edge, it immediately turns the corresponding switch OFF while simultaneously starting a programmable-delay counter for the complementary switch. Only after this dead-time delay has elapsed is the complementary switch commanded to turn ON. This module is the fundamental building block responsible for generating two safe, non-overlapping final output signals (HS and LS) for one phase.
dead_zone_inserter.v (The "Manager" Module): This module serves as a high-level structural wrapper that manages the dead-time for the entire three-phase system. Its primary role is to provide a clean interface to the rest of the motor controller. Internally, its logic is very simple: it instantiates three separate copies of the dead_zone_channel.v worker module—one for phase U, one for phase V, and one for phase W. It takes the three ideal High-Side signals from the main PWM generator and the global dead_zone_value from the control registers and correctly routes them to the respective child instances. This design ensures that each phase is handled independently and encapsulates the complexity, presenting a simple "dead-time insertion" block to the top-level design.
This two-tiered strategy allowed us to first rigorously test the complex timing logic of a single channel in isolation (unit testing) before integrating it into the full three-phase system, leading to a more robust and verifiable design.
